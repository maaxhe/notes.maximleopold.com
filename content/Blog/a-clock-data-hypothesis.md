Here I propose a short theoretical piece written for the Cognitive Science Master Course _Current Topics in Brain Connectivity_ at the University of Trento.

I came up with a speculative idea: that the brain’s two major attention streams, dorsal (“where”) and ventral (“what”), might be coordinated not just anatomically, but **rhythmically**.

In 2015, Pascal Fries introduced the _Communication-Through-Coherence_ (CTC) theory, suggesting that brain areas communicate effectively only when their oscillations are aligned.  
He also proposed that slower rhythms like theta can reset faster ones, such as gamma, helping to organise neural communication in time.

Later, when reading Soyuhos et al. (2022), I noticed something intriguing: the posterior Inferior Frontal Junction (IFJp) showed theta-band directionality toward both the Frontal Eye Field (FEF) and the anterior IFJ. That looked suspiciously like coordination to me.

That was the birth of my **Clock-and-Data Hypothesis**.

I wondered: what if theta rhythms from IFJp act like a **clock**, assigning time slots for different types of attention?  
What if FEF (routing spatial information) and IFJa (routing object-based information) each get their own theta phase to avoid crosstalk?

It’s a speculative idea, but one I found worth writing down.

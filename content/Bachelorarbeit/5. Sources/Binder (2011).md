## Binder (2011)

**Core argument:** A Story > Math fMRI contrast provides a reliable, multicenter-validated method for mapping anterior temporal lobe (ATL) language regions. Story comprehension (auditory short narratives from Aesop's fables, followed by 2-AFC comprehension questions) reliably activates bilateral ATL, including lateral, inferior, and polar temporal regions, which a semantic arithmetic control task does not.

**Methods:**
- Story task: short Aesop's fable stories presented via MRI-compatible headphones, followed by a 2-alternative forced-choice question testing story content
- Math task: active attentional control — serial addition/subtraction problems, 2-AFC response (used to cancel non-semantic auditory/attentional activations)
- Initial cohort N = 18 at Medical College of Wisconsin; validated across 6 further sites (N = 77 total across 7 US centers)
- Analysis: Story > Math contrast at group level; cluster threshold p < .005 corrected

**Key results:**
- Story > Math robustly activated lateral and inferior ATL bilaterally, as well as medial temporal regions
- Results replicated across all 7 imaging centers despite differences in scanner vendor, field strength, and head coil
- Established this paradigm as a reliable functional localizer for language/semantic regions in the ATL

**Why relevant to thesis:**
The HCP Language Task (used in §3.4.2.1 / §4.6.1 for behavioral prediction of 'what'-stream efficiency) is directly derived from this Binder et al. (2011) paradigm — same story stimuli (Aesop's fables), same 2-AFC question format, same Story > Math contrast logic. Barch et al. (2013) cite Binder et al. (2011) explicitly as the source of the HCP Language Task design.

**Relevance to thesis:**
- Cite in §3.4.2.1 as the source for the HCP Language Story task design
- The story accuracy and story reaction time measures used for the 'what'-stream behavioral prediction derive their validity from this normative study

# 1.3 Hypothesis, A supramodal organization
The preceding sections have outlined a clear asymmetry: while the dual-stream architecture of the auditory cortex is well established, the prefrontal regions that coordinate these streams from the top down remain poorly defined. For the visual system, this question has been resolved - FEF and IFJa act as dissociable control hubs for the dorsal and ventral streams ([[Bedini & Baldauf (2021)]]), whether a comparable organization exists for the auditory streams is still unknown. 

We hypothesize that the resting-state functional connectivity of the auditory what and where streams shows a clear dissociation: the where-stream preferentially connects to the Frontal Eye Field (FEF), and the what-stream to the anterior Inferior Frontal Junction (IFJa).

Given that FEF and IFJa are established directors of top-down attentional control in the visual domain (Bedini & Baldauf, 2021), we propose that they may function as an analogous role for auditory attention, FEF along the where pathway, IFJa along the what pathway.

Long-term, we hypothesize that this dissociation reflects a supramodal organizational principle of the prefrontal cortex, with FEF and IFJa acting as domain-general hubs for top-down attentional control across sensory modalities. This study tests a first step toward that larger claim: whether the prefrontal cortex shows the same functional division in the auditory domain as has been established for the visual domain.

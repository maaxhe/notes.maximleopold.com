# 3.2 Selection of Regions of Interest (ROIs)
To test the hypothesis of supramodal prefrontal control over auditory processing, we defined a comprehensive set of 30 target ROIs and 3 seed regions per hemisphere. All regions were identified using the multimodal cortical parcellation (**HCP-MMP1.0**) by [[Glasser et al. (2016) - Nature]]. This atlas provides significantly higher neuroanatomical precision than traditional Brodmann maps by integrating structural (myelin), functional (task-fMRI), and connectivity data.

ROIs were divided into three functional categories: (1) Prefrontal Seed Regions (the hypothesized controllers), (2) Target regions of the Auditory What-Stream (ventral/semantic), and (3) Target regions of the Auditory Where-Stream (dorsal/spatial).

## 3.2.1 Prefrontal Seed Regions 
We selected distinct prefrontal "control hubs" as seed regions based on the functional dissociation previously described in the visual system by [[Bedini & Baldauf (2021)]]. Under the supramodal hypothesis, we investigate whether these visual control architectures map onto auditory processing.

- **Frontal Eye Field ([[FEF]]):** Located in the caudal middle frontal gyrus, ventral to the junction of the superior precentral sulcus (sPCS) and superior frontal sulcus (SFS). The FEF contains topographic maps of contralateral space and is a core node of the Dorsal Attention Network ([[DAN]]), responsible for spatial attentional control. 
- **anterior Inferior Frontal Junction ([[IFJ]]a):** Located at the junction of the inferior precentral sulcus (iPCS) and the inferior frontal sulcus (IFS). Unlike the FEF, the IFJa is part of the Frontoparietal Network ([[FPN]]) and functions as a mediator for lower-order, feature-based regions, particularly in language-related networks.
- **posterior Inferior Frontal Junction ([[IFJ]]p - Control Seed):** While the majority of neuroimaging literature treats the IFJ as a monolithic unit, recent structural and functional evidence suggests a clear dissociation between its anterior (IFJa) and posterior (IFJp) subdivisions ([[Bedini (2023) - Brain Structure]]). IFJp acts as a core node of the multiple-demand system, activated by generalized executive tasks (e.g., reasoning, math). We included IFJp specifically as a control region to validate the target specificity of the IFJa towards the auditory what-pathway.

## 3.2.2 Auditory What-Stream Targets (Ventral)
The auditory what-stream is primarily associated with object recognition and, in humans, semantic and speech processing. Based on the hierarchical organization of the auditory cortex mapped by the Glasser parcellation ([[Glasser et al. (2016) - Nature]]) and effective connectivity studies ([[Rolls et al. (2023) - Cerebral Cortex]]), we defined the following specific trajectory for the ventral pathway:

### Early Auditory Processing (The Origin)
- **Primary Auditory Cortex:** The fundamental acoustic processing for both pathways originates in the primary auditory cortex ([[A1]]) located on Heschl's Gyrus, alongside early adjacent regions such as Area 52.
- **The Ventral Branching Point:** The functional divergence into the specialized ventral what-stream initiates in the anterolateral belt and parabelt regions. According to [[Rolls et al. (2023) - Cerebral Cortex]], area [[A4]] acts as a critical transition node within this early hierarchy, establishing the first strong anterior projections.

### Anterior Temporal Pathway
- **Anterior Temporal Nodes:** Following the initial branching from A4, the signal propagates anteriorly. Area A4 shows preferential connectivity to the anterior temporal region [[TA2]]. This region is tightly coupled with the anterior superior temporal gyrus ([[STGa]]), forming a distinct anterior routing for auditory signals.
- **Semantic Integration:** The core of the human auditory what-stream is formed by the multimodal, semantic areas of the superior temporal sulcus. We selected [[STSda]] and [[STSdp]], which receive substantial input from the early anterior pathway (TA2, STGa) and serve as crucial hubs before the pathway projects further towards the inferior frontal cortex.

### High-Level Convergence
- **Temporoparietooccipital Junction:** Areas [[TPOJ1]], [[TPOJ2]], and [[TPOJ3]] were included as high-level association cortices. While anatomically posterior, these regions act as semantic convergence zones during complex language comprehension tasks, bridging the ventral temporal stream with broader cognitive networks.

## 3.2.3 Auditory Where-Stream Targets (Dorsal)



The auditory where-stream processes spatial localization and sensorimotor integration. Given that research on exact prefrontal auditory dorsal pathways is less exhaustive than its visual counterpart, target selection heavily relied on the connectivity profiles outlined by [[Rolls et al. (2023) - Cerebral Cortex]].

- **Frontal Opercular Area 1 ([[fOP]]1):** The frontal operculum is a primary target of the auditory dorsal pathway ([[Frühholz (2015) - NeuroImage#^5b4da1]]). FOP1 captures the anterior endpoint of the "Sound-to-Motor" stream defined by [[Hickok & Poeppel (2004) - Cognition]]. Furthermore, it connects to the "Group 3" network ([[A4]], [[A5]]), linking it to somatosensory processing.
- **Spatial Supporting Regions ([[7AL]], [[7Am]], [[7PC]]):** These superior parietal regions are heavily involved in spatial processing. Under our supramodal hypothesis—where prefrontal areas like the FEF direct both visual and auditory spatial attention—it is highly plausible that these parietal regions serve as multimodal spatial maps for auditory targets as well.
- **Control/Ambiguous Regions:** - **[[MT]] / [[MST]]:** While crucial for visual motion, evidence suggests no direct response to purely auditory stimuli in these areas (Siehe [[MT#Source]]), making them an important control boundary.
  - **[[PSL]]:** While linked to language-semantic functions ([[Rolls et al. (2023) - Cerebral Cortex]]), recent data shows PSL is unresponsive to basic auditory stimuli ([[Dureux (2024)#^51288d]]). Its high connectivity to the STS complex (along with TPOJ1) suggests a highly specialized role closer to the ventral stream, requiring further clarification.

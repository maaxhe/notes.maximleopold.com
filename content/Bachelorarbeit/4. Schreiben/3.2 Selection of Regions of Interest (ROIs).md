# 3.2 Selection of Regions of Interest (ROIs)
To test the hypothesis of supramodal prefrontal control over auditory processing, a comprehensive set of 36 target ROIs and 2 primary seed regions (plus one control seed) per hemisphere was defined. All regions were identified utilizing the multimodal cortical parcellation (**HCP-MMP1.0**) by [[Glasser et al. (2016) - Nature]]. This atlas provides superior neuroanatomical precision by integrating structural, functional, and connectivity data.

The selected ROIs are categorized into functional networks reflecting the auditory dual-stream architecture and their respective prefrontal controllers. The large sample size (N=812) provides sufficient statistical power to detect moderate connectivity effects across all ROI pairs after multiple comparison correction
## 3.2.1 Prefrontal Seed Regions (The Conductors)
We selected distinct prefrontal control hubs based on the functional dissociation previously established in the visual system by [[Bedini & Baldauf (2021)]]. This selection tests whether these visual control architectures map onto auditory processing.

- **Frontal Eye Field (FEF):** A core node of the Dorsal Attention Network (DAN), hypothesized to exert top-down control over the spatial auditory "Where"-stream.
- **Anterior Inferior Frontal Junction (IFJa):** Part of the Frontoparietal Network (FPN), hypothesized to mediate feature-based attention and semantic processing within the auditory "What"-stream [[Bedini & Baldauf (2021)]].
- **Posterior Inferior Frontal Junction (IFJp) - Control Seed:** Included strictly as a methodological baseline. IFJp is associated with the Multiple-Demand (MD) system for general-purpose executive tasks [[Bedini & Baldauf (2021)]]. Factoring out its variance allows for a double dissociation, ensuring that the observed control over the auditory network is highly specific to the IFJa.

## 3.2.2 The Dorsal Pathways (Where & How)

The dorsal pathways process spatial localization (Where) and sensorimotor integration (How) ([[Hickok & Poeppel 2007 - Nature]]). ROI selection relies on the connectivity profiles outlined by [[Rolls et al. (2023) - Cerebral Cortex]] and recent functional data by [[Dureux (2024)]].

### The Spatial-Orienting Network (Where-Stream)

This network tracks moving auditory objects and directs spatial focus.
- **7AL, 7Am, 7PC:** Superior parietal areas receiving direct input from early auditory regions, crucial for auditory spatial attention and motion tracking [[Rolls et al. (2023) - Cerebral Cortex]].
- **PF, PFop:** Inferior parietal areas within the task-positive network serving as somatosensory integration hubs to align auditory signals with body orientation [[Caspers / Glasser et al. (2016)]].
    
- **PFcm:** Included as a methodological boundary node of the parietal spatial network [[Glasser et al. (2016) - Nature]], despite recent evidence suggesting an absence of direct auditory responsiveness [[Dureux (2024)]].
    
- **MT, MST:** Classical supramodal motion hubs. They receive effective connectivity from auditory areas (A4/A5) to enable the tracking of auditory movement in space [[Rolls et al. (2023) - Cerebral Cortex]].
    

### The Articulatory-Motor Interface (How-Stream)

This interface translates auditory representations into articulatory motor plans (Sound-to-Motor mapping).

- **55b:** A premotor hub exhibiting rapid responses to vocal stimuli, functioning as a relay station for linguistic actions [[Glasser et al. (2016) - Nature]]; [[Dureux (2024)]].
    
- **44 (Broca's pars opercularis):** The classical frontal endpoint of the dorsal pathway, responsible for phonological processing and articulatory planning [[Hickok & Poeppel (2004) - Cognition]], [[Hickok & Poeppel 2007 - Nature]]; [[Rolls et al. (2023) - Cerebral Cortex]].
    
- **OP4, FOP1, FOP2, FOP3:** The frontal operculum serves as a primary target for affective prosody and action planning [[Frühholz (2015) - NeuroImage]]. OP4 specifically demonstrates exclusive sensitivity to human vocalizations [[Dureux (2024)]].

## 3.2.3 The Ventral Pathways (What)

The ventral stream decodes auditory object identity, semantics, and speech perception.

### Ventral Semantic and Identity Network

- **STGa, STSda, STSdp, STSva, STSvp, TA2:** The semantic core of the ventral pathway processing complex auditory objects. The STS complex integrates vocal inputs with facial motor representations to decode identity and message [[Glasser et al. (2016)]]; [[Rolls et al. (2022/2023)]].
    
- **AVI, SCEF:** The Anterior Ventral Insular and Supplementary and Cingulate Eye Field exhibit highly specific capabilities in distinguishing vocalizations from noise, extending the semantic network into insular and medial evaluation regions [[Dureux et al. (2024)]].
    
- **45, 47l, IFSp:** The frontal termini of the ventral meaning pathway. Area 45 (Broca's pars triangularis) and 47l handle semantic selection, while IFSp orchestrates top-down feature-based working memory [[Bedini & Baldauf (2021)]]; [[Rolls et al. (2023)]].
    

### Anterior Temporal Semantic Periphery

- **TE1a, TGd, TGv:** Located at the temporal pole, these areas form the absolute endpoint of the ventral hierarchy. They represent the "Sound-Meaning-Interface" where auditory information is consolidated into supramodal, abstract conceptual knowledge [[Rolls et al. (2022/2023)]].

## 3.2.4 Hierarchical Gateways and Supramodal Connectors

These regions serve as routing hubs and convergence zones between early acoustic analysis and higher-order integration.

- **PBelt, A4, A5:** The primary "routers" exiting the auditory core. A4 projects into the ventral path, while A5 acts as a hybrid node triggering both dorsal motion (MT/MST) and ventral semantic networks [[Rolls et al. (2023)]].
    
- **PSL, STV, PGi, TPOJ1:** Multimodal convergence zones (Group 3 networks) bridging auditory semantics with visual and somatosensory inputs [[Rolls et al. (2022/2023)]]. Notably, while PSL is anatomically integrated into the semantic network, functional models suggest it acts as an abstract linguistic interface rather than a primary auditory responder [[Dureux et al. (2024)]].

# 3.2 Selection of Regions of Interest (ROIs)
To test the hypothesis of supramodal prefrontal control over auditory processing, we defined a comprehensive set of 30 target ROIs and 3 seed regions per hemisphere. All regions were identified using the multimodal cortical parcellation (**HCP-MMP1.0**) by [[Glasser et al. (2016) - Nature]]. This atlas provides significantly higher neuroanatomical precision than traditional Brodmann maps by integrating structural (myelin), functional (task-fMRI), and connectivity data.

ROIs were divided into three functional categories: (1) Prefrontal Seed Regions (the hypothesized controllers), (2) Target regions of the Auditory What-Stream (ventral/semantic), and (3) Target regions of the Auditory Where-Stream (dorsal/spatial).

## 3.2.1 Prefrontal Seed Regions 
We selected distinct prefrontal "control hubs" as seed regions based on the functional dissociation previously described in the visual system by [[Bedini & Baldauf (2021)]]. Under the supramodal hypothesis, we investigate whether these visual control architectures map onto auditory processing.

- **Frontal Eye Field ([[FEF]]):** A core node of the Dorsal Attention Network ([[DAN]]), hypothesized to control the spatial "Where"-stream.
- **anterior Inferior Frontal Junction ([[IFJ]]a):** Part of the Frontoparietal Network ([[FPN]]), hypothesized to mediate feature-based attention and semantic processing in the "What"-stream. ([[Bedini & Baldauf (2021)]])
- **posterior Inferior Frontal Junction ([[IFJ]]p - Control Seed):** Included to validate the functional specificity of IFJa. IFJp is associated with the Multiple-Demand (MD) system and general-purpose executive tasks, serving as a boundary for auditory-specific control.

## 3.2.2 Auditory What-Stream Targets (Ventral)
The auditory what-stream is primarily associated with object recognition and, in humans, semantic and speech processing. Based on the hierarchical organization of the auditory cortex mapped by the Glasser parcellation ([[Glasser et al. (2016) - Nature]]) and effective connectivity studies ([[Rolls et al. (2023) - Cerebral Cortex]]), we defined the following specific trajectory for the ventral pathway:

- **Early Processing:** [[A1]] (Primary Auditory), [[A4]] and [[A5]]  These regions represent the transition from spectrotemporal analysis to specialized streams.
- 
- **Semantic Hubs:** The Superior Temporal Sulcus complex (**STSda, STSdp, STSva, STSvp**) and the anterior Superior Temporal Gyrus (**STGa**).

- **Integration Nodes:** **TA2** (Temporal Area 2), **PSL** (Perisylvian Language Area), and the **TPOJ** cluster (**TPOJ1, TPOJ2, TPOJ3**). TPOJ serves as a high-level convergence zone for semantic representations.

- **Frontal Terminals:** Areas **44, 45,** and **47l**, representing the anterior terminus of the ventral pathway.

## 3.2.3 Auditory Where-Stream Targets (Dorsal)



The auditory where-stream processes spatial localization and sensorimotor integration. Given that research on exact prefrontal auditory dorsal pathways is less exhaustive than its visual counterpart, target selection heavily relied on the connectivity profiles outlined by [[Rolls et al. (2023) - Cerebral Cortex]].

- **Frontal Opercular Area 1 ([[fOP]]1):** The frontal operculum is a primary target of the auditory dorsal pathway ([[Frühholz (2015) - NeuroImage#^5b4da1]]). FOP1 captures the anterior endpoint of the "Sound-to-Motor" stream defined by [[Hickok & Poeppel (2004) - Cognition]]. Furthermore, it connects to the "Group 3" network ([[A4]], [[A5]]), linking it to somatosensory processing.
- **Spatial Supporting Regions ([[7AL]], [[7Am]], [[7PC]]):** These superior parietal regions are heavily involved in spatial processing. Under our supramodal hypothesis—where prefrontal areas like the FEF direct both visual and auditory spatial attention—it is highly plausible that these parietal regions serve as multimodal spatial maps for auditory targets as well.
- **Control/Ambiguous Regions:** - **[[MT]] / [[MST]]:** While crucial for visual motion, evidence suggests no direct response to purely auditory stimuli in these areas (Siehe [[MT#Source]]), making them an important control boundary.
  - **[[PSL]]:** While linked to language-semantic functions ([[Rolls et al. (2023) - Cerebral Cortex]]), recent data shows PSL is unresponsive to basic auditory stimuli ([[Dureux (2024)#^51288d]]). Its high connectivity to the STS complex (along with TPOJ1) suggests a highly specialized role closer to the ventral stream, requiring further clarification.

# 5.4 Anatomical Ambiguities

## 5.4.1 Areas A4 and A5
We initially assigned A4 and A5 to the where-stream based on conflicting evidence in the literature (Section 3.2.5), but partial correlation analysis resolves this ambiguity empirically: both areas show robust bilateral coupling with IFJa and no meaningful coupling with FEF (Section 4.4.1). The MT/MST projections and BA44 connections reported by Rolls et al. (2023) do not contradict this classification. Area 44 is itself embedded in the IFJa-controlled language network (Section 4.3.2), and MT/MST connectivity does not produce FEF coupling in partial correlation. Glasser et al. (2016) further place both areas in auditory association cortex activated in the Language Story contrast, with strong IFG connectivity. This positions A4 and A5 as early gateways through which acoustic input enters IFJa-governed identity processing.

## 5.4.2 PSL
No other ROI in our analysis displays the connectivity profile found for PSL: bilateral IFJa coupling combined with an exclusively right-lateralized FEF connection, entirely absent in the left hemisphere (Section 4.4.2). Dureux (2024) reports that PSL is entirely unresponsive to auditory input across all stimulus categories, suggesting it operates at an abstract, modality-independent level above acoustic processing. Rolls et al. (2023) further confirm directional effective connectivity from STS regions into PSL, consistent with a high-level convergence role driven by fully abstracted inputs. This suggests PSL functions as a bilateral semantic node via IFJa that additionally integrates right-hemisphere spatial context via FEF, a cross-stream profile not observed in any other region of the analysis.

## 5.4.3 STV
STV couples directly and comparably with both prefrontal seeds in their respective single-seed analyses, after partialling out the other (Section 4.4.3). The near-identical coupling magnitudes make unambiguous stream assignment on this basis alone impossible. Rolls et al. (2023) classify STV within the ventral language network, and Glasser et al. (2016) report its deactivation in language-task contrasts, pointing toward a primary what-stream affiliation with secondary dorsal access. As a visual area in the posterior STS, its coupling with two auditory prefrontal hubs is notable. Rather than invalidating the analysis, this suggests that both attentional systems access STV as a site of audiovisual convergence — consistent with its position at the posterior STS, which is consistently linked to multisensory integration (Rolls et al., 2023). STV's dual coupling therefore likely reflects its role as a multimodal interface rather than membership in either auditory stream per se.

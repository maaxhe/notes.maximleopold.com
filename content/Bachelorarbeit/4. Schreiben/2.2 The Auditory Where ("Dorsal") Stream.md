# 2.2 The Auditory Where ("Dorsal") Stream

## 2.2.1. Spatial Processing and Audio-Motor Integration
The functional role of the auditory dorsal stream has been subject to ongoing debate. Early hypotheses characterized it as a "where" system responsible for spatial hearing, supported by studies on non-human primates ([[Rauschecker & Afsahi (2023) - Journal of Comparative Neurology]]), neuroimaging showing right-lateralized activation in the temporo-parietal cortex for spatial processing, as well as spatial neglect is more severe after damage in the right hemisphere. 
The Inferior Parietal Lobe (IPL) does not seem to be driven by acoustic stimuli ([[Rauschecker & Scott (2009) - Nature Neuroscience]]), but still the angular gyrus is involved in higher-order speech processing - along with clear prefrontal activation. This suggests, the inferior parietal cortex is involved in domain-general, language-related speech comprehension, rather than low-level auditory processing ([[Rauschecker & Scott (2009) - Nature Neuroscience]]).
Ahveninen et al. (2006) reports a double dissociation between anterior "what" and posterior "where" stream responding at 70 - 150ms after stimulus onset. Rauschecker (2011) further argued that monkeys use the dorsal stream for spatial processing, whereas humans use it for sensorimotor integration in speech. 

Hickok and Poeppel (2004, 2007) instead proposed that the dorsal stream maps acoustic speech signals into articulatory-based representations, projecting from the STG via Sylvian parietal temporal (Spt) to frontal regions, including Broca's area. Therefore, suggesting the dorsal pathway being critical for speech production rather than comprehension. For the purposes of this thesis, we treat the dorsal stream as a **spatial-motor system** subserving both functions, consistent with a synthesis of both models.
## 2.2.2 Anatomical Definition and Evidence
The auditory dorsal "where"-stream originates posterior to Herschl's Gyrus in the planum temporale and STG ([[Ahveninen et al. (2006) - PNAS]]), and extends through auditory association areas before reaching parietal and frontal regions. A4 and A5 have EC to motion-sensitive areas MT and MST, which connect to superior parietal regions (7AL, 7Am, 7PC), consistent with a dorsal "where"-stream ([[Rolls et al. (2023) - Cerebral Cortex]]).
Further, the stream is characterized by Rolls' (2022) **Group 3**, with connectivity to auditory belt regions, motion-sensitive visual areas, and somatosensory cortex. 

The **Frontal** **Eye** **Field** **(FEF)**, located in the caudal middle frontal gyrus ventral to the junction of the superior precentral sulcus (sPCS) and superior frontal sulcus (SFS), serves as a primary prefrontal node of the dorsal stream. The FEF contains a full topographic map of contrallateral space, and is a core node of the Dorsal Attention Network (DAN), mediating covert spatial attention, oculomotor control, and spatial working memory ([[Bedini & Baldauf (2021)]]). It is structurally connected via SLF1 and SLF2 to posterior parietal and temporoparietal cortices.

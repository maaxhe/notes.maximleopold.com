# 4.2 Testing the "Where" Stream (FEF Connectivity)
FEF shows strong coupling with motion areas MT/MST, motor areas as FOP1 and early auditory areas. Our partial correlation analysis reveals that FEF is functionally connected to the spatial areas in parietal cortex (7Am, 7PC) and motion areas MT, MST, Belt region PBelt and to TGd in the temporal lobe in the left hemisphere. The right hemisphere shows a very different partial correlation, connecting FEF to 7Am, MST, PSL and TGd. 

- spatial anchors: The FEF maintains direct coupling with the superior parietal lobule 
## 4.2.1 Full correlation
An initial analysis using standard full correlation suggested a massive, unspecific dorsal network. In thus model, the left FEF exhibited highly significant coupling across almost the entore parietal and superior temporal cortex. For instance, it shoed strong connections to early auditory transitions areas like A4 ($z = 9.93$ ) and the superior temporal vestibular area [[STV]] ($z = 8.04$), alongside massive coupling with parietal nodes (7Am, $z = 8.36$; 7PC, $z = 5.81$, PFcm, $z = 24.07$). 
Relying solely on full correlation could lead to false conclusions that the FEF exerts direct top-down control over a broad auditory where-dtream that extends deepl into the temporal lobe. However, applying regularized parrtial correlation revealed that muchof this temporal connectivity was driven by indirect network covariance.

### 4.2.2 Partial correlation 
Isolating the unique variance via partial correlation pruned the network, withdrawing the FEF alsmost entorely from the temporal lobe and revealing a highly specific dorsal architecture. 

In the left hemisphere, the FEF maintians direct, robust functional coupling with the superior parietal lobule, specifically areas 7Am ($z = 8.36$) and 7PC. These regions form the core structure of spatial orienting. Withiung the realy auditory cortex itself, the direct infleunce of the FEF is remarkably narrow, targeting only the parabelt region (PBelt, $z= 2.92$). This suggests that early auditory spatial features are forwarded to the parietal cortex, where the FEF exerts top-down spatial control.

## 4.2.2 Auditory Space
Regarding the auditory cortex, the partial correlation data reveal that the FEF does not broadly interact with the temporal lobe. Instead, it selectively targets highly specific nodes to extract spatial and action-relevant acoustic information.

- **The Early Gateway:** The FEF bypasses the primary auditory core almost entirely. The only direct connection to early auditory processing is found in the parabelt region (**PBelt**, $z = 2.90$ in the left hemisphere). This indicates that the FEF retrieves auditory spatial cues only after initial spectrotemporal analysis is completed, preventing top-down interference during low-level acoustic feature extraction.
    
- **The Opercular "How" Interface:** The most robust prefrontal-auditory coupling occurs at the frontal operculum bilaterally (**FOP1**, $z = 12.72$ left, $z = 15.34$ right; and **FOP3**). These regions act as the anterior anchors of the dorsal "how" stream. The massive connectivity from the FEF suggests a direct mechanism for translating auditory spatial coordinates into oculomotor and pre-motor action maps.
    
- **Lateralized Ambiguities:** The strict separation of the dual-stream model shows signs of intermodal blurring in the partial correlation data. While the left FEF connects to **TA2** ($z = 3.79$), the right FEF establishes a strong connection to the Perisylvian Language Area (**PSL**, $z = 12.62$). Both targets are traditionally associated with ventral language processing. Furthermore, bilateral connections to the temporal pole regions (**TE1a**, **TGd**) were observed. Rather than falsifying the supramodal hypothesis, these connections likely represent necessary spatial-semantic integration—allowing the FEF to assign spatial coordinates to complex auditory objects identified by the ventral stream.

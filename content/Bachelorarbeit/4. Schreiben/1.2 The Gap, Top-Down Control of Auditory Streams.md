# 1.2 The Gap, Top-Down Control of Auditory Streams
The division into dorsal and ventral pathways is well-documented([[Ahveninen et al. (2006) - PNAS]], [[Hickok & Poeppel 2007 - Nature]]). However, describing both pathways leaves a critical question unanswered: How are these streams top-down directed? 

In environments with competing sounds, the brain cannot attend to all auditory input simultaneously. Top-down attention functions as a control mechanism that filters relevant information and suppresses distractors - therefore enabling goal-directed listening ([[De Vries & Baldauf (2021) - Journal of Neuroscience]]). The key question is: *Which prefrontal regions act as the top-down controllers of these auditory streams?* 

In the visual system, this question has been answered. Recent neuroimaging research has identified the double-association in the prefrontal cortex, conducted by the FEF and IFJ: The former is a dorsal region specialized for spatial processing and oculomotor control. It performs a strong top-down influence over the visual where ("dorsal") stream ([[Bedini & Baldauf (2021)]], [[Bedini (2023) - Brain Structure]]). The latter (IFJa) is a region located ventrally from the FEF and controls the visual wha "ventral" stream specializing on object-based attention ([[Bedini & Baldauf (2021)]]). This dissociation demonstrates a clear "control hub" hierarchy for vision. For the auditory system, the exact top-down "control hubs" are less defined. 

For the auditory system, an equivalent picture does not yet exist. Existing work has identified IFG subregions as frontal nodes in auditory processing, particularly BA44 and BA45 within the semantic pathway ([[Rolls et al. (2023) - Cerebral Cortex]]) and BA44 also along the dorsal pathway for affective prosody ([[Frühholz (2015) - NeuroImage]]). However, these findings remain specific to the semantic what-stream or dorsal prosody processing. The auditory where-stream lacks a clear prefrontal controller: [[Hickok & Poeppel 2007 - Nature]] described a dorsal pathway including the Spt - a region at the parietotemporal boundary within the Sylvian fissure - as a sensorimotor interface, connecting anteriorly to Broca's region and premotor cortex. [[Hickok & Poeppel 2007 - Nature#^277549]]. The question of whether the FEF and IFJa also direct the auditory domain in a similar fashion to the visual system remains unanswered.

The dual-stream framework introduced in sections 2.1   

  and 2.2 raises a fundamental question for this thesis: 

  are the prefrontal nodes of the auditory "what"- and   

  "where"-streams modality-specific, or do they reflect a

   domain-general organizational principle shared with 

  other sensory systems?

  

  Evidence from the visual domain suggests the latter.   

  The **Frontal Eye Field (FEF)** and the **Inferior Frontal** 

  **Junction (IFJa)** are two prefrontal structures that have

   been established as the primary nodes of top-down   

  attention control in the visual system — with a clear

  functional dissociation between them. The FEF

  predominantly processes spatial information, mediates

  covert spatial attention, oculomotor control, and

  spatial working memory, and is a core node of the

  Dorsal Attention Network ([[Bedini & Baldauf (2021)]]).

   The IFJa, by contrast, is selective for non-spatial,

  feature- and object-based information and shows

  stronger connectivity with the ventral "what"-pathway

  ([[Bedini (2023) - Brain Structure]]). Critically,

  resting-state MEG data show stronger connectivity of

  the FEF with the dorsal visual "where"-pathway and

  stronger connectivity of the IFJ with the ventral

  visual "what"-pathway ([[Bedini (2023) - Brain

  Structure]]).

  

  If this spatial/non-spatial division of top-down       

  attention control in the prefrontal cortex is truly

  **supramodal** — not tied to the visual system specifically

   — then the same FEF–IFJa dissociation should be     

  reflected in auditory connectivity as well: the FEF

  preferentially connected to the auditory

  "where"-stream, and the IFJa preferentially connected

  to the auditory "what"-stream. This is the central

  hypothesis tested in the present thesis.
